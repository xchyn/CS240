Practice and resources

Exercises to work through, software you need to install, and the handful of web tools worth keeping in a browser tab. One page per topic, added as the semester goes on.

Every exercise carries a full worked solution, hidden until you click. Printing a page opens all of them.

Exercise sets

Each set follows a topic we have covered in class. Work the problems on paper first — the solution is one click away, and reading it before you have tried is the fastest way to feel confident about something you cannot actually do.

  1. 1Morse code: encoding, decoding and timingReading and writing the code; the dit–dah timing rules; words per minute and the PARIS standard; how long a message actually takes to send. 15 exercises.
  2. 2Number bases and conversionPositional notation in any base; decimal to base b and back; binary, quaternary, octal and hexadecimal, and the grouping shortcut that connects them. 15 exercises.
More coming

Sets for ASCII and character codes, gates and Boolean algebra, truth tables and circuits, K-maps, signed integers, floating point and MIPS will appear here as we reach them.

How to use this page

Try, then check

Every exercise is a click-to-open box. The solution shows the whole method, not just the answer, so you can find the exact step where your work diverged.

Print it

Printing a page — or saving it as a PDF — opens every solution and drops the navigation. That gives you a clean problem set with a full answer key.

Dark mode

The ◖ button in the top right switches between light and dark, and remembers your choice.
